**GBP/USD** — the British pound against the US dollar — is one of the most actively traded pairs in forex. Its old nickname, **"cable,"** dates back to the transatlantic telegraph cable that once carried exchange rates between London and New York.

## What the pair represents

GBP/USD tells you how many US dollars it takes to buy one British pound. If GBP/USD is 1.27, then £1 = $1.27.

- The **pound** is the base currency
- The **US dollar** is the quote currency

A rising pair means the pound is strengthening; a falling pair means the dollar is winning.

## Why cable is more volatile

GBP/USD tends to move more sharply than steadier pairs like EUR/USD. Several factors drive this:

- The UK economy is smaller and more sensitive to shifts in sentiment
- The pound reacts strongly to Bank of England surprises
- Political events have repeatedly produced large, fast moves
- Liquidity, while deep, is thinner than the euro-dollar market

## The main drivers

### 1. Central bank divergence

The core story is the **Bank of England versus the Federal Reserve**. When the BoE is raising rates while the Fed holds, the pound tends to strengthen — and vice versa. The interest-rate gap is the engine.

### 2. UK economic data

Inflation, employment, wage growth and GDP all shift expectations for BoE policy and move the pound quickly.

### 3. US dollar strength

Because the dollar is one half of the pair, broad dollar moves — driven by Fed policy or global risk sentiment — affect GBP/USD regardless of what's happening in the UK.

### 4. Risk sentiment and politics

In risk-off periods, capital often flows to the dollar, pressuring cable. UK political developments can add sudden volatility.

| Driver | Effect on GBP/USD |
| --- | --- |
| BoE more hawkish than Fed | Tends to push higher |
| Strong US data / hawkish Fed | Tends to push lower |
| Global risk-off | Often lower (dollar strength) |
| Positive UK data surprise | Often higher |

## When it's most active

Like most majors, GBP/USD is busiest during the **London and New York session overlap**, when both markets are open and liquidity peaks.

## The bottom line

GBP/USD is a rate-differential story with extra volatility baked in. Watch the Bank of England against the Fed, respect that cable can move fast on surprises, and size positions accordingly. That volatility is why traders love it — and why it demands discipline.
