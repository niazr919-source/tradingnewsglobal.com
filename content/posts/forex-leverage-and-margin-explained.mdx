Leverage is the most powerful — and most dangerous — feature in forex trading. It's also the single biggest reason new traders lose accounts quickly. Understanding it properly is not optional.

## What leverage actually is

Leverage lets you control a position much larger than your own capital. It's expressed as a ratio:

- **1:30** — $1,000 controls $30,000
- **1:100** — $1,000 controls $100,000
- **1:500** — $1,000 controls $500,000

Your broker effectively lends you the difference. Regulated brokers in many regions cap retail leverage (often around 1:30) precisely because higher ratios cause so much harm.

## Margin: the deposit you put up

**Margin** is the portion of your own money set aside to open the position. It isn't a fee — it's collateral.

At 1:100 leverage, opening a $100,000 position requires **$1,000** of margin (1%).

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Used margin | Capital locked in open positions |
| Free margin | What remains available to trade or absorb losses |
| Margin level | Equity ÷ used margin, as a percentage |
| Margin call | Broker warning that your equity is running low |
| Stop out | Broker forcibly closes positions to prevent further loss |

## Why it cuts both ways

Here's the maths that people underestimate. With a $100,000 position:

- A **1% move in your favour** = +$1,000
- A **1% move against you** = −$1,000

If your account holds $1,000, that 1% adverse move is **your entire account**. The market only has to breathe against you.

> Leverage does not increase your edge. It only increases the size of the outcome — in both directions.

## The margin call spiral

As losses mount, free margin shrinks. Once the margin level falls below your broker's threshold, you get a **margin call**. If it keeps falling, the broker triggers a **stop out**, closing positions automatically at whatever price is available.

This often happens at the worst possible moment — during volatile news, when spreads widen and slippage is worst.

## Using leverage sensibly

1. **Risk a fixed small percentage** per trade (commonly 1–2%), regardless of available leverage
2. **Always use a stop-loss** — decide your exit before entering
3. **Size positions from your risk**, not from what the broker permits
4. **Reduce size around major news** when volatility spikes
5. Remember that **available leverage is a limit, not a target**

## A useful reframe

Think of high leverage as a *permission*, not an instruction. A broker offering 1:500 is not suggesting you use it. Professional traders typically operate at a small fraction of the leverage available to them, because survival matters more than any single trade.

## The bottom line

Leverage magnifies everything — profits, losses, and mistakes. The traders who last are the ones who treat it as a risk-control problem rather than an opportunity to trade bigger. Size small, use stops, and let consistency compound.
