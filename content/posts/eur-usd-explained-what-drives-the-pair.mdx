If you've spent any time around forex, you've seen **EUR/USD** everywhere. It's the most traded currency pair in the world — and a great place for beginners to start. Here's what it actually means and what moves it.

## What EUR/USD represents

EUR/USD tells you how many **US dollars** it takes to buy **one euro**. If EUR/USD is 1.08, then €1 = $1.08.

- The **euro** is the *base* currency
- The **US dollar** is the *quote* currency

When the pair rises, the euro is strengthening against the dollar. When it falls, the dollar is strengthening.

## Why it's so popular with traders

| Feature | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Deep liquidity | Easy to enter and exit, even in size |
| Tight spreads | Lower trading costs |
| Rich data | Two major economies, endless analysis |
| Predictable sessions | Most active during London–New York overlap |

## The main drivers

### 1. Central bank policy

The single biggest factor. The interest-rate gap between the **Federal Reserve** and the **European Central Bank** pushes the pair up or down. A stronger Fed stance tends to lift the dollar and pressure EUR/USD.

### 2. Economic data

Inflation reports, jobs numbers, GDP and manufacturing surveys from both regions shift expectations for future rates.

### 3. Risk sentiment

In times of global fear, investors often rush to the dollar as a safe haven, weighing on EUR/USD.

### 4. Politics and events

Elections, policy shifts and geopolitical tension can all inject volatility.

> Think of EUR/USD as a tug-of-war: the euro on one side, the dollar on the other. Whichever economy looks stronger pulls the rope its way.

## When it's most active

EUR/USD moves most during the **London and New York session overlap** (roughly early-to-midday US time), when liquidity and volume peak.

## The bottom line

EUR/USD is popular for good reason: it's liquid, cheap to trade, and driven by well-understood forces — mainly the Fed-versus-ECB story. Master this one pair and you'll understand the logic behind most of the forex market.
