> **Risk disclaimer:** Short-term and binary trading are high-risk activities. Most retail traders lose money. This article is educational only and is not financial advice.

Most beginners obsess over entries. Experienced traders obsess over **risk**. The reason is simple arithmetic: you cannot trade an account you've already lost.

## Rule 1: Fixed fractional risk

Risk the same small percentage of your account on every trade — commonly **1–2%**.

With a $2,000 account and 1% risk, your maximum loss per trade is **$20**. That's true whether you feel confident or uncertain, because confidence is a feeling, not an edge.

This approach has a useful property: as your account grows, position sizes grow with it. As it shrinks, they shrink automatically, slowing the bleeding during a bad run.

## Rule 2: Understand drawdown maths

Losses are not symmetrical with gains. This table is worth memorising:

| Account loss | Gain needed to recover |
| --- | --- |
| 10% | 11% |
| 25% | 33% |
| 50% | 100% |
| 75% | 300% |
| 90% | 900% |

> Losing half your account means you must **double** what's left just to get back to even. This is why capital preservation comes before profit.

## Rule 3: Know your expectancy

Expectancy tells you whether a strategy makes money over many trades:

**Expectancy = (Win rate × Average win) − (Loss rate × Average loss)**

If the result is positive, the strategy is profitable over a large sample. If it's negative, no amount of discipline or position sizing will save it — you're simply losing more slowly.

### For fixed-payout instruments

With binary options the maths is stricter, because a loss costs 100% of the stake while a win pays less:

**Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + payout)**

At an 80% payout, you need roughly a **56%** win rate just to break even. Anything less and the structure grinds your account down regardless of how good individual trades feel.

## Rule 4: Set session limits

Decide **before** you start:

1. **Daily loss limit** — e.g. 3% of the account. Hit it, stop for the day.
2. **Daily profit target** — protects gains from being given back.
3. **Maximum trades per session** — prevents revenge trading and overtrading.

The purpose of these limits is to remove decisions from the moment when your judgement is worst: immediately after a loss.

## Rule 5: Keep a trading journal

Record every trade: setup, size, outcome, and your emotional state. After 50–100 trades, patterns emerge that are invisible day to day — often revealing that a small number of impulsive trades caused most of the damage.

## Putting it together

- Risk 1% per trade, always
- Know the break-even threshold for your instrument
- Stop at your daily limit, no exceptions
- Journal everything and review weekly

## The bottom line

Risk management is not the boring part of trading — it *is* trading. Your strategy determines your edge; your risk rules determine whether you survive long enough to realise it.
