You'll hear "bull market" and "bear market" constantly across crypto, forex and stocks. They describe not just direction, but the **mood** of a market — and understanding the difference shapes how you think about risk.

## The definitions

- **Bull market:** prices are generally rising, optimism dominates, and buyers are in control. A common informal threshold is a sustained rise of 20% or more from recent lows.
- **Bear market:** prices are generally falling, pessimism dominates, and sellers are in control — often defined as a decline of 20% or more from recent highs.

The names are said to come from how each animal attacks: a bull thrusts its horns **up**, a bear swipes its paws **down**.

## What each phase feels like

| | Bull market | Bear market |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mood | Optimism, greed | Fear, caution |
| Behaviour | Buying dips, FOMO | Selling rallies, capitulation |
| Risk | Complacency | Paralysis |
| Danger | Overpaying | Panic-selling lows |

> The greatest risk in a bull market is thinking you're a genius. The greatest risk in a bear market is thinking it will never end. Both are the same mistake: assuming the current mood is permanent.

## What drives the cycle

Markets move in cycles because human emotion does. Rising prices attract buyers, which pushes prices higher, which attracts more buyers — until valuations outrun reality. The reverse happens on the way down. Layered on top are real fundamentals: interest rates, liquidity, regulation and, in crypto, adoption and technology cycles.

## Why the mindset matters more than the label

Trying to perfectly time the top or bottom is a losing game — even professionals rarely manage it. What matters more is adjusting your **behaviour** to conditions:

- In euphoric bull phases, guard against overpaying and oversizing
- In fearful bear phases, guard against panic-selling and abandoning your plan
- In both, stick to consistent risk rules rather than chasing the mood

## A practical takeaway

You don't need to predict the cycle to survive it. You need to:

1. Recognise which mood dominates
2. Resist doing what everyone else is doing emotionally
3. Keep your position sizing consistent regardless of the phase

## The bottom line

Bull and bear markets are the rhythm of every asset class, driven by the same human emotions that trip up traders individually. You can't control the cycle, but you can control your response to it — and that response is what determines your results over the long run.
