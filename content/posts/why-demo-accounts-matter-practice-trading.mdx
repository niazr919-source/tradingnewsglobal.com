A **demo account** (also called paper trading) lets you trade with virtual money using live market prices. It's the single most valuable tool for a beginner — and one of the most misused. Here's how to get real value from it.

## What a demo account is for

A demo replicates a real trading platform with fake funds. Its purpose is to let you make your beginner mistakes where they cost nothing.

Use it to learn:

- **Platform mechanics** — placing orders, setting stops, reading charts
- **Strategy testing** — does your approach actually work over many trades?
- **Position sizing** — practising risk rules until they're automatic
- **Routine** — building a repeatable pre-trade process

## How to use one properly

The mistake most people make is treating demo money as meaningless. If you don't take it seriously, it teaches nothing.

1. **Trade the same size you'll trade live.** If your real account will be $1,000, set the demo to $1,000 — not $100,000.
2. **Follow your risk rules exactly.** Risk 1% per trade, use stops, respect daily limits.
3. **Keep a journal from day one.** Track every trade and review it.
4. **Set a graduation bar.** For example: a documented positive track record over 50–100 trades before risking real money.

> Demo trading with $100,000 of pretend money teaches you nothing except how to feel confident. Trade it like it's real, or it isn't practice.

## What a demo cannot teach you

Be honest about the limits. Demo accounts remove the one thing that changes everything: **real emotion**.

When actual money is at stake, fear and greed behave differently. Traders who are calm and disciplined on demo often fall apart on their first live trades. That gap is normal — and it's why the transition to live should be gradual.

## Making the transition

When you do go live:

- Start with the **smallest position sizes possible** (micro lots, minimum stakes)
- Expect your performance to dip at first as emotions kick in
- Treat the early live phase as **continued practice**, not a profit push
- Scale up only after you've proven you can hold discipline with real money

## A realistic timeline

There's no rush. Many successful traders spend months on demo before going live, then months more trading tiny sizes. Compare that to the alternative — losing a real account in weeks — and the patience pays for itself.

## The bottom line

A demo account is free tuition. Used seriously — same size, same rules, same journal — it builds the mechanics and discipline that protect your capital later. Used carelessly, it just builds false confidence. The difference is entirely in how you treat it.
