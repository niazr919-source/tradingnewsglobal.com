Two numbers dominate every crypto listing page: **market capitalization** and **24-hour volume**. They look similar and are constantly confused — but they measure completely different things, and misreading them is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

## Market cap: what a project is "worth"

Market capitalization is simply:

**Market cap = current price × circulating supply**

It estimates the total value of all coins currently in circulation. It's useful for comparing the relative size of projects — a $50 billion asset and a $5 million asset are in entirely different risk categories.

### The circulating supply trap

Here's what trips people up. Market cap only counts coins **in circulation**. Many projects lock up large portions of supply for teams, investors or future release.

That leads to a second metric: **Fully Diluted Valuation (FDV)**, which uses the *maximum* supply instead.

| Metric | Formula | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market cap | Price × circulating supply | Value of coins available now |
| FDV | Price × total supply | Value if every coin existed today |

> If a token's FDV is many times its market cap, a large amount of supply is still waiting to be unlocked. That future supply can put heavy downward pressure on price.

## Volume: how much is actually trading

Trading volume measures the **value exchanged** over a period, usually 24 hours. It tells you about liquidity and interest, not value.

High volume generally means:

- You can enter and exit positions more easily
- Spreads are tighter and slippage is lower
- The price is more likely to reflect genuine supply and demand

Low volume means the opposite — and a low-volume asset can be moved dramatically by a single sizeable order.

## The ratio that reveals a lot

Compare volume to market cap. A healthy, actively traded asset usually sees meaningful daily volume relative to its size. Warning signs include:

- **Very low volume, very high market cap** — the valuation may be illusory, propped up by thin trading
- **Volume far exceeding market cap** — sometimes genuine excitement, sometimes artificial "wash trading" designed to fake activity

## Applying it in practice

1. Check **circulating vs total supply** before judging a valuation
2. Look at whether volume is spread across **several reputable exchanges** or concentrated on one
3. Ask *why* a metric moved — a market cap rise from real buying is very different from one caused by newly unlocked tokens
4. Never treat "low market cap" as automatic upside; it usually just means higher risk

## The bottom line

Market cap tells you **size**. Volume tells you **liquidity**. Neither tells you whether something is a good investment. Read them together, always check supply structure, and be suspicious of numbers that don't line up.
