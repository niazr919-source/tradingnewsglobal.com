Before you can manage risk in forex, you need to speak its language. Two terms do most of the work: **pips** and **lots**. Together they tell you exactly how much money a price move is worth.

## What is a pip?

A **pip** ("percentage in point") is the standard smallest price move for most currency pairs — the **fourth decimal place**.

If EUR/USD moves from 1.0850 to 1.0851, that's **one pip**.

The exception is pairs involving the Japanese yen, which are quoted to two decimals. For USD/JPY, a move from 151.20 to 151.21 is one pip.

You may also see a **pipette** — one tenth of a pip, shown as a fifth decimal (1.08505). Brokers use these for finer pricing.

## Lot sizes: how much you're actually trading

A **lot** is the number of units of the base currency you're trading. Your lot size determines what each pip is worth.

| Lot type | Units | Approx. pip value* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | 100,000 | $10 per pip |
| Mini | 10,000 | $1 per pip |
| Micro | 1,000 | $0.10 per pip |
| Nano | 100 | $0.01 per pip |

\* For pairs quoted with USD as the second currency, such as EUR/USD.

## Putting it together

Say you buy **one mini lot** of EUR/USD at 1.0850 and it rises to 1.0870.

- Move: 1.0870 − 1.0850 = **20 pips**
- Pip value: **$1** per pip (mini lot)
- Result: 20 × $1 = **$20 profit**

Had you traded a standard lot, the same 20-pip move would be **$200**. Same market, same idea — ten times the risk.

> Your lot size, not your prediction, is what decides how much a mistake costs you.

## Why this matters for risk

This is the practical use of pips. Suppose you have a $2,000 account and you're willing to risk **1%** ($20) on a trade, with a stop-loss 20 pips away.

1. Risk allowed: $20
2. Stop distance: 20 pips
3. Maximum pip value: $20 ÷ 20 = **$1 per pip**
4. That means: **one mini lot**

Work in that order — risk first, then position size. Most beginners do it backwards, picking a lot size and discovering their risk afterwards.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Trading standard lots on a small account "to make it worthwhile"
- Ignoring that pip value shifts on pairs where USD isn't the quote currency
- Forgetting that **spread** costs you pips on every single trade
- Widening a stop-loss instead of reducing lot size

## The bottom line

A pip measures the move; the lot size decides what that move is worth to you. Master this relationship and position sizing becomes arithmetic instead of guesswork — which is exactly what separates disciplined traders from gamblers.
