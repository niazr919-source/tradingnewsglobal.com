Gold has been a store of value for thousands of years, and it still trades actively today across futures, spot and ETF markets. For traders, the key question is simple: **what actually moves its price?**

## Gold pays no interest — and that's the key

Unlike a bond or a savings account, gold produces no yield. That single fact explains most of its behaviour. When you hold gold, you give up the interest you could have earned elsewhere. So gold competes directly with interest-bearing assets.

This leads to the most important driver:

## 1. Real interest rates

**Real interest rate = nominal rate − inflation.** This is the single biggest influence on gold.

- **Rising real rates** make interest-bearing assets more attractive → gold tends to fall
- **Falling or negative real rates** reduce the opportunity cost of holding gold → gold tends to rise

This is why gold reacts so strongly to Federal Reserve policy and inflation data.

## 2. The US dollar

Gold is priced in dollars globally, so the two often move inversely:

- A **stronger dollar** makes gold more expensive for other currencies → can pressure the price
- A **weaker dollar** tends to support gold

## 3. Fear and safe-haven demand

Gold is the classic "safe haven." During market panic, geopolitical crises or banking stress, investors often move into gold precisely because it isn't tied to any government or company's promise. This demand can push prices up even when interest-rate logic suggests otherwise.

## 4. Inflation and currency debasement

Over long periods, gold is seen as a hedge against the erosion of paper-currency value. When confidence in fiat currencies falls, demand for gold often rises.

## 5. Central bank buying

Central banks hold gold as part of their reserves, and their buying or selling can meaningfully affect long-term demand.

| Driver | Gold tends to... |
| --- | --- |
| Rising real interest rates | Fall |
| Falling / negative real rates | Rise |
| Stronger US dollar | Fall |
| Market fear / crisis | Rise |
| Rising inflation expectations | Rise |

> Gold's contradictions usually resolve into one question: what is happening to **real interest rates**? Start there, then layer on the dollar and fear.

## Why it behaves differently from stocks

Gold often moves independently of equities, which is exactly why investors use it for diversification. When stocks fall in a panic, gold frequently holds up or rises — though not always, especially when investors sell everything for cash.

## The bottom line

Gold isn't mysterious once you understand it pays no yield. Watch real interest rates first, the dollar second, and fear third. Together those three forces explain the vast majority of what moves the world's oldest safe-haven asset.
