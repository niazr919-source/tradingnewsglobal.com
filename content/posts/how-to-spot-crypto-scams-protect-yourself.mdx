Crypto's speed and irreversibility make it a favourite target for fraud. Once funds leave your wallet, there is usually no way to get them back. Recognising the common schemes is your best defence.

## The most common scams

### Rug pulls

Developers launch a token, attract investment, then abandon the project and drain the funds. Warning signs: anonymous teams, unrealistic promised returns, and liquidity that isn't locked.

### Phishing

Fake websites, emails or messages designed to steal your login details or seed phrase. Often a near-perfect copy of a real exchange or wallet, sometimes promoted through paid search ads.

### Fake giveaways

"Send 1 coin, receive 2 back" — often impersonating well-known figures or brands. No legitimate giveaway ever requires you to send crypto first.

### Pig butchering

A long-con where a scammer builds a relationship (often romantic or friendly) over weeks, then lures the victim into a fake investment platform showing fake profits — until they try to withdraw.

### Fake support

Scammers posing as customer service in chat groups, reaching out first to "help." Real support will never DM you first or ask for your seed phrase.

## Universal red flags

> If you only remember one thing: **guaranteed returns do not exist**, and **no one legitimate will ever ask for your seed phrase**.

Be suspicious whenever you see:

- Promises of guaranteed or unusually high returns
- Pressure to act quickly ("limited time," "spots filling up")
- Requests for your seed phrase or private keys
- Unsolicited DMs offering help or opportunities
- Anonymous teams with no verifiable track record
- Requests to send crypto to "unlock" a withdrawal

## Practical protection habits

1. **Never share your seed phrase** — with anyone, for any reason
2. **Bookmark official sites** and only access wallets/exchanges through them
3. **Verify contract addresses** from official sources before interacting
4. **Ignore unsolicited offers** — legitimate opportunities don't arrive in your DMs
5. **Use a hardware wallet** for meaningful holdings
6. **Slow down** — urgency is a manipulation tactic; real opportunities survive a night's sleep

## If something feels off

Trust that instinct. Disconnect your wallet, stop engaging, and verify independently through official channels. Scammers rely on emotion and speed — removing both defeats most of them.

## The bottom line

The best protection in crypto isn't technical, it's behavioural: guard your seed phrase, distrust guaranteed returns, and never let urgency rush your decisions. In a system with no chargebacks, prevention is the only real safety net.
