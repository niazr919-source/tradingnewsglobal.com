An **economic calendar** lists scheduled data releases and central bank events, along with their expected impact. It's the closest thing forex has to a weather forecast — it won't tell you which way the wind blows, but it tells you when the storm arrives.

## What a calendar entry shows you

Every release typically includes four pieces of information:

| Column | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Time | When the data is published (check your time zone) |
| Impact | Expected volatility, usually low / medium / high |
| Forecast | What economists collectively expect |
| Previous | Last period's reading |
| Actual | The real number, published at release time |

The market has already priced in the **forecast**. What moves price is the **difference between actual and forecast** — the surprise.

## The releases that matter most

Not all data is equal. These consistently move currency markets:

- **Central bank rate decisions** — the Fed, ECB, Bank of England and others
- **Inflation (CPI)** — drives expectations for future rate moves
- **Employment data** — especially US Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP)
- **GDP** — the broad health of an economy
- **Retail sales and PMI surveys** — timely signals of momentum

> A "high impact" tag doesn't mean the market will move a lot. It means it *can*. If the actual number matches the forecast closely, price may barely react.

## How to actually use it

### 1. Check it before your session

Know what's scheduled for the currencies you trade, and at what times. Being surprised by a release you could have anticipated is an avoidable mistake.

### 2. Understand the reaction, not just the number

Sometimes strong data produces a weak currency move — because traders had expected something even stronger, or because attention shifted to a different part of the report. Read the market's response, don't assume it.

### 3. Respect the mechanics of news volatility

Around major releases:

- **Spreads widen** significantly
- **Slippage** becomes likely — you may not get your requested price
- **Whipsaws** are common: an initial spike that fully reverses within minutes

## Three practical approaches

1. **Stand aside.** The most underrated choice. No position, no risk.
2. **Reduce size.** Trade smaller so widened spreads and volatility don't hurt.
3. **Wait for the dust to settle.** Let the first move complete, then trade the direction that holds.

What experienced traders almost never do is hold a large, leveraged position into a major release with a tight stop-loss — volatility will often take the stop out before the real move begins.

## The bottom line

An economic calendar doesn't predict direction, but it removes the excuse of being blindsided. Check it daily, know which releases affect your pairs, and treat high-impact windows as a risk-management decision rather than a trading opportunity.
