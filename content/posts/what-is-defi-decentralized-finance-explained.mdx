**Decentralized finance** — DeFi — is an attempt to rebuild traditional financial services using blockchain code instead of banks, brokers and clearing houses. Instead of applying to a lender, you interact with a smart contract that executes automatically.

## The core idea

In traditional finance, an institution sits in the middle of every transaction. It holds your money, verifies your identity, and takes a fee. DeFi replaces that middleman with **open-source software running on a blockchain**.

The result is a financial system that is:

- **Permissionless** — anyone with a wallet can use it
- **Transparent** — the code and transactions are public
- **Automated** — rules execute without human approval

## What you can actually do in DeFi

### Lending and borrowing

You deposit crypto into a pool and earn interest from borrowers. Borrowers post collateral — usually *more* than they borrow — and if the collateral's value drops too far, it is automatically sold to repay the loan. This is called **liquidation**.

### Decentralized exchanges (DEXs)

Rather than matching buyers with sellers through an order book, most DEXs use **liquidity pools**. Users deposit pairs of tokens into a pool, and traders swap against that pool. Depositors earn a share of the trading fees.

### Yield farming

Moving capital between protocols to chase the highest returns. High advertised yields almost always signal high risk — often because a protocol is paying out its own newly issued token.

## Where the risk actually lives

| Risk | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Smart contract bugs | Code flaws can be exploited and funds drained |
| Liquidation | A price drop can wipe out your collateral automatically |
| Impermanent loss | Providing liquidity can leave you worse off than just holding |
| Rug pulls | Anonymous teams abandoning a project and taking funds |
| Regulatory uncertainty | Rules are still being written in most countries |

> In DeFi there is no customer support line and no chargeback. If funds are lost to a bug or a mistaken transaction, they are usually gone permanently.

## Understanding impermanent loss

This one catches out most newcomers. When you deposit two tokens into a liquidity pool and their prices move apart, the pool automatically rebalances your holdings. You can end up with less value than if you had simply held the two tokens in your wallet. Trading fees may or may not make up the difference.

## A sensible way to start

1. Learn on a **testnet** or with a very small amount first
2. Prefer protocols that have been **audited** and running for years
3. Understand exactly how liquidation works before borrowing
4. Never invest based on an advertised APY alone

## The bottom line

DeFi is a genuine innovation — programmable, transparent, always-on financial infrastructure. But it moves the risk from institutions onto **you**. The returns are real, and so are the losses. Learn the mechanics before committing meaningful capital.
