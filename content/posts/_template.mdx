Open with the point. State what this article explains and why it matters, in two or three
sentences. Avoid throat-clearing about how important the topic is.

## First section heading

Body content in **Markdown**, with GitHub-flavoured extensions: lists, tables,
blockquotes and code blocks.

Every `##` and `###` heading is added automatically to the table of contents with a
matching anchor, so write headings a reader could navigate by.

| Column | Column |
| --- | --- |
| Tables scroll horizontally on mobile | Keep them to four columns or fewer |

> Blockquotes are styled as pull-out callouts. Use one for the single sentence you most
> want a skimming reader to take away.

## Second section heading

House style notes:

- **Risk goes in the body, not only in the footer.** Where a product loses money for most
  retail traders, say so and cite the regulator.
- **No price targets, no signals, no buy-now framing.** See the editorial policy.
- **Attribute numbers.** If a figure matters, it belongs in `sources`.
- **Prefer the mechanism over the narrative.** Explain why something happens, not just that
  it did.
- **Aim for 1,000 words or more.** Thin content is the most common reason a finance site
  fails an ad-network or search quality review.

## The bottom line

Close with what the reader should actually take away. One short paragraph.

*This article is educational and is not financial advice.*
