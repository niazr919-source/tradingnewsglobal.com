A **stablecoin** is a cryptocurrency designed to hold a steady value — most commonly pegged to one US dollar. They act as the cash layer of crypto markets, letting traders move in and out of volatility without leaving the blockchain.

But "stable" is a design goal, not a guarantee. How a stablecoin maintains its peg determines how much you should trust it.

## The three main models

### 1. Fiat-backed (reserve-backed)

The issuer holds real dollars, Treasury bills and cash equivalents in bank accounts. Each token is meant to be redeemable for $1.

- **Strength:** simple, well understood, historically the most robust
- **Weakness:** you must trust the issuer's reserves and their auditors

### 2. Crypto-backed (overcollateralized)

Backed by other cryptocurrencies locked in smart contracts. Because crypto is volatile, these are **overcollateralized** — you might lock $150 of crypto to mint $100 of stablecoin.

- **Strength:** transparent, verifiable on-chain
- **Weakness:** sharp market crashes can trigger mass liquidations

### 3. Algorithmic

Attempts to hold the peg through supply-adjusting mechanisms and incentives, often with little or no hard collateral.

- **Strength:** capital efficient in theory
- **Weakness:** historically fragile — several have collapsed entirely

| Model | Backed by | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fiat-backed | Cash & short-term bonds | Issuer / custodial trust |
| Crypto-backed | Overcollateralized crypto | Liquidation cascades |
| Algorithmic | Code & incentives | Peg collapse ("death spiral") |

## What a "depeg" means

A depeg happens when a stablecoin trades away from its target — say $0.97 instead of $1.00. Causes include:

- Doubts about whether reserves genuinely exist
- A rush of redemptions the issuer can't meet quickly
- Problems at a partner bank or custodian
- Collateral collapsing faster than the system can liquidate

> Even brief depegs matter. If you're using a stablecoin as collateral in DeFi, a small drop can trigger liquidations across your positions.

## What to check before trusting one

1. **Reserve transparency** — are there regular, credible attestations?
2. **What backs it** — cash and Treasuries are safer than exotic assets
3. **Redemption rights** — can holders actually redeem for dollars?
4. **Regulatory standing** — increasingly, jurisdictions require licensing
5. **Track record** — has it held its peg through past market stress?

## Why they matter beyond trading

Stablecoins are becoming genuine payment rails — fast, cheap cross-border transfers that settle in minutes. They're also the natural settlement layer for tokenized real-world assets, which is why regulators worldwide are now writing rules for them.

## The bottom line

Stablecoins are infrastructure, not investments. Their value comes from reliability, so choose based on **transparency and backing**, not on yield offers. And remember: a stablecoin is only as strong as whatever stands behind it.
