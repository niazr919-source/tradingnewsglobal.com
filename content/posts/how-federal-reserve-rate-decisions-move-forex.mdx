Few events move the currency market like a **Federal Reserve interest rate decision**. Understanding *why* rates matter — and what traders actually react to — is essential for anyone trading forex.

## Why interest rates move currencies

At its core, a currency's value is tied to the return investors can earn holding it. When a central bank raises interest rates:

- Assets in that currency pay more
- Global capital flows toward it
- Demand for the currency tends to rise

Lower rates do the opposite. This is why the **US dollar** often strengthens when the Fed is hawkish (leaning toward higher rates) and weakens when it turns dovish.

## It's expectations, not just the decision

Here is the part beginners miss: markets price in what they *expect* long before the announcement. The reaction depends on the **surprise** relative to expectations.

> A rate hike that everyone expected can actually weaken a currency if the Fed hints that it's the last one.

So traders watch three things:

1. **The decision itself** — hike, hold, or cut
2. **The statement** — the language and tone
3. **The projections ("dot plot")** — where officials see rates heading

## The tools traders watch

| Signal | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| FOMC statement | Immediate policy stance |
| Dot plot | The future rate path |
| Press conference | Nuance and Q&A surprises |
| Economic data | Inflation and jobs that shape the next move |

## How this hits pairs like EUR/USD

EUR/USD is essentially a bet on the Fed versus the European Central Bank. If the Fed is raising while the ECB holds, the **rate differential** favors the dollar, and EUR/USD tends to fall. When that gap narrows, the pair often rises.

## Trading around Fed events — carefully

- Volatility spikes and spreads widen around the announcement
- The first move often reverses once the press conference begins
- Position sizing matters more than picking a direction

## The bottom line

The Fed doesn't just set a number — it sets expectations for the future path of money. Learn to read the *tone* and the *projections*, not just the headline rate, and you'll understand why the dollar moves the way it does.
